# N-027｜Uniform Digit-Sum Ratios over Subsets

## 题面

Let $S(k)$ denote the sum of all digits of $k$ in base 10. Find all integers $n \ge 2$ and rational numbers $\beta \in (0,1)$, such that there exist $n$ distinct positive integers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ that satisfy: for any subset $I \subseteq \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ with two or more elements,
$$ S\left(\sum_{i \in I} a_i\right) = \beta \cdot \sum_{i \in I} S(a_i). $$

## 答案

All integers n from 2 to 10 inclusive, with any rational beta in the open interval (0, 1).

## 解法要点

The desired are integers $n \in \{2, 3, \dots, 10\}$ and all rational numbers $\beta \in (0, 1)$.

First, for integer $n \in \{2, 3, \dots, 10\}$ and rational $\beta \in (0, 1)$, construct $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ as follows. Let $c$ and $s$ be positive integers that will be determined later. For $k = 1, \dots, n$, define
$$
a_k = 2 \cdot 10^{cn+sk} - 10^{cn+s(k-1)+1} + \sum_{\substack{i \in \{1, \dots, n\} \\ i \neq k}} 10^{cn+s(i-1)+1} + 10^{k-1} \cdot \sum_{i=0}^{c-1} 10^{ni}
$$
Here, among the last $cn$ places of $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$, each place corresponds to a unique $a_i$ whose digit at that place is nonzero, and that digit is 1; for the next (to the left) $s$ places, $a_1$ has $199\dots9$ ($s-1$ of 9's), while all other $a_i$'s have $00\dots01$; for the next $s$ places, $a_2$ has $199\dots9$ ($s-1$ of 9's), while all other $a_i$'s have $00\dots01$; and so on.

For every $1 \le i \le n$, $S(a_i) = n + 9(s - 1) + c$; for every
$$
I \subseteq \{1, 2, \dots, n\} \ (|I| \ge 2),
$$
it is easy to check that
$$
S\left(\sum_{i \in I} a_i\right) = n \cdot |I| + c \cdot |I|.
$$
As long as $s, c \in \mathbb{N}_+$ are suitably chosen such that
$$
(n + 9(s - 1) + c) \cdot \beta = n + c,
$$
the problem requirements are all met.

Next, we prove $n \le 10$. Assume $n = 11$. If
$$
I_1, I_2 \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}
$$
satisfy $I_1 \cap I_2 = \emptyset$ and $|I_1| \ge 2$, $|I_2| \ge 2$, then
$$
S\left(\sum_{i \in I_1 \cup I_2} a_i\right) = \beta \cdot \sum_{i \in I_1 \cup I_2} S(a_i) = S\left(\sum_{i \in I_1} a_i\right) + S\left(\sum_{i \in I_2} a_i\right).
$$
In other words, no carry occurs in the summing of $\sum_{i \in I_1} a_i$ and $\sum_{i \in I_2} a_i$. However, from $\beta < 1$ we see that a carry occurs in the summing of $a_i$ and $a_j$, for every $1 \le i < j \le 11$.

Suppose $T$ is the least positive integer that the summing $a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_{11}$ involves a carry in the $T$th place (from the right). Let the $T$th place digits of $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_{11}$ be $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_{11}$, respectively. For any subset
$$
I \subseteq \{1, 2, \ldots, 11\},
$$
the $T$th place digit of $\sum_{i \in I} a_i$ is the ones place digit of $\sum_{i \in I} x_i$. Without loss of generality, assume
$$
9 \ge x_1 \ge x_2 \ge \cdots \ge x_{11} \ge 0.
$$
Then $x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_{11} \ge 10$. We assert that there exist $I_1, I_2 \subseteq \{1, 2, \ldots, 11\}$, $I_1 \cap I_2 = \emptyset$, $|I_1| \ge 2$, $|I_2| \ge 2$, such that the sum of the ones place digits of $\sum_{i \in I_1} x_i$ and $\sum_{i \in I_2} x_i$ is greater than or equal to 10.

If $x_{11} = 0$, starting at $x_{10} + x_9$, add one number at a time by $x_8, x_7, \ldots, x_1$. There must be a time that a carry occurs in the ones place: for some $k \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 8\}$, the summing $(x_{10}+x_9+\cdots+x_{k+1})+x_k$ involves a carry in the ones place, and so does $(x_{10} + x_9 + \cdots + x_{k+1}) + (x_k + x_{11})$, a contradiction.

If $x_{11} > 0$, try to make $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_{11}$ into as many pairs as possible, such that the sum of each pair ends with a digit greater than or equal to 2. If five pairs are made, then the summing involves a carry in the ones place, a contradiction. Assume at most four pairs are made, leaving three or more unpaired numbers, which include at most one number less than 5 and one number greater than 5, as otherwise two such numbers can be paired. This indicates there is at least one 5, and furthermore, no unpaired number is less than 5, and hence there are at least two 5s, say $x_a$ and $x_b$. Take the third unpaired number $x_c \ge 5$. Now, starting at any $x_d+x_e$ (not involving $x_a, x_b, x_c$), add one number at a time by the other numbers except $x_a, x_b$. Since $x_c \ge 5$, the summing must involve a carry in the ones place at some moment, say $(\sum_{i} x_{i}) + x_{f}$. Then we are led to contradiction by taking
$$ (\sum_{i} x_{i}) + (x_{f} + x_{a} + x_{b}). $$

Based on the above argument, when $n \ge 11$, no positive integers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ satisfy the problem condition. $\square$

## 提示阶梯

1. 分别处理存在性构造与规模上界：构造时要人为安排十进制数位块中的进位，上界则关注数字和下降所强迫的进位。
2. 构造中让每个数拥有互不重叠的低位 1，并在各自的高位块放置 1 后跟若干个 9；上界中取总和发生进位的最右数位。
3. 令该最右数位上的数字降序排列，并把它们分成两个各至少含两项的不交子集，使两个子和的个位数相加产生进位；这与不交子集数字和应可加矛盾。构造端则调节两个块长，使单项数字和与任意多项和的数字和满足给定比例。
