# G-003｜Disjoint Discs Covering Lattice

## 题面

A square grid on the Euclidean plane consists of all points $(m, n)$, where $m$ and $n$ are integers. Is it possible to cover all grid points by an infinite family of discs with non-overlapping interiors if each disc in the family has radius at least $5$?

## 答案

No

## 解法要点

It is not possible. The proof is by contradiction. Suppose that such a covering family $\mathcal{F}$ exists. Let $D(P, \rho)$ denote the disc with center $P$ and radius $\rho$. Start with an arbitrary disc $D(O, r)$ that does not overlap any member of $\mathcal{F}$. Then $D(O, r)$ covers no grid point. Take the disc $D(O, r)$ to be maximal in the sense that any further enlargement would cause it to violate the non-overlap condition. Then $D(O, r)$ is tangent to at least three discs in $\mathcal{F}$. Observe that there must be two of the three tangent discs, say $D(A, a)$ and $D(B, b)$, such that $\angle AOB \le 120^\circ$. By the Law of Cosines applied to triangle $ABO$,
$$
(a+b)^2 \le (a+r)^2 + (b+r)^2 + (a+r)(b+r),
$$
which yields
$$
ab \le 3(a+b)r + 3r^2, \quad \text{and thus}\quad 12r^2 \ge (a-3r)(b-3r).
$$
Note that $r < 1/\sqrt{2}$ because $D(O, r)$ covers no grid point, and $(a-3r)(b-3r) \ge (5-3r)^2$ because each disc in $\mathcal{F}$ has radius at least $5$. Hence $2\sqrt{3}r \ge (5-3r)$, which gives $5 \le (3+2\sqrt{3})r < (3+2\sqrt{3})/\sqrt{2}$ and thus $5\sqrt{2} < 3+2\sqrt{3}$. Squaring both sides of this inequality yields $50 < 21+12\sqrt{3} < 21+12 \cdot 2 = 45$. This contradiction completes the proof.

## 提示阶梯

1. 作反证：在给定圆盘之间寻找一个不含格点的极大空圆盘，用它与周围圆盘的相切关系制造半径矛盾。
2. 极大空圆至少与三个给定圆相切，其中有两个圆心对空圆圆心所成角不超过 $120°$；对这三个圆心组成的三角形应用余弦关系。
3. 若空圆半径为 $r$、两相邻给定圆半径为 $a,b$，推出 $12r^2≥(a-3r)(b-3r)$。再用空圆不含格点给出的 $r<1/√2$ 和 $a,b≥5$ 代入，化简后平方比较。
